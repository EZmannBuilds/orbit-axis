Privacy Policy

Effective 21 July 2026 · Last updated 21 August 2026

Orbit Axis calculates astrology charts from your birth details. Those details are personal, and this page says exactly what is stored, why, who else can see it, and how to remove all of it.

On this page

    Who publishes Orbit Axis

    Orbit Axis is published by not yet published. For privacy questions, contact not yet published.

    What you provide

    • Email address and password — to create and access your account. Passwords are handled by Supabase Auth and are never stored by Orbit in readable form.
    • Birth date, birth time, and birthplace — the inputs a chart is calculated from.
    • Coordinates and time zone — derived from the birthplace you select, so historical daylight-saving rules can be applied correctly.
    • Chart names and saved charts — including charts you create for other people.
    • Preferences — such as your detail level.
    • Questions you ask Ask Orbit, and the conversations they belong to.
    • Anything you send to support.

    If you create a chart for somebody else, you are providing their birth details. Please only do that for people who would be comfortable with it.

    What Orbit Axis calculates from it

    • Natal chart placements, houses, angles, and aspects
    • Transits, synastry, and the current sky
    • Daily readings, the evidence behind them, and your reading history
    • Ask Orbit answers and the evidence they were built from
    • Which chart is active, and when you last selected it

    These are stored so your charts and readings are the same on every device you sign in from.

    Technical information

    Orbit keeps deliberately little:

    • Session information — an authentication cookie that keeps you signed in. It cannot be read by scripts in your browser.
    • Request identifiers — a random per-request id used to trace an error you report. It is not linked to you.
    • Error information — the error code and route. Request bodies are never logged, so your birth details do not appear in Orbit's logs.
    • Standard server request data handled by the hosting provider.
    • Kept in your browser, not on Orbit's servers — your theme and display preferences, whether you have turned over today's Tarot card, and — when you are signed in — whether today's card has already been added to your reflections, so it is not sent twice. The Tarot marker records only that you revealed the card and for which date; the card itself is worked out from the date each time, so it is the same card whatever device you open. Nothing in this group is sent to Orbit, and clearing your browser storage removes it. Orbit also keeps two random identifiers in browser storage for the usage counts described below; unlike this group, those two are sent to Orbit, and clearing your browser storage removes them as well.

    There is no advertising, no tracking pixel, and no third-party profiling. Orbit does not sell or share your data, because there is nobody to share it with.

    Understanding how Orbit is used

    Orbit previously collected nothing at all about how it was used. That has changed, and this section says exactly how, because a privacy page that quietly stops being true is worse than one that never made the promise.

    Orbit now counts a small number of things about its own use, on its own servers. This is first-party only: no third-party analytics service, no SDK, no advertising network, and nothing that can follow you to another site.

    What is recorded

    • A visit — when it happened, the page you landed on, and the website that linked you, recorded as a host name such as instagram.com rather than a full address.
    • Campaign labels — if you followed a link Orbit published, the utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content labels in it, plus an identifier for the Orbit post the link came from. Only those five are read; the rest of the address is ignored, and they are removed from your address bar once read.
    • A short list of actions — that a screen was opened (Today, Sky, Atlas, Tarot, Compatibility), that an account was created, that a chart was created, that a reflection was saved, and that a visit was a return. Each is stored as a name and a time, nothing else.
    • Two random identifiers that your browser generates for itself and stores: one for the visit, and one that persists so Orbit can tell whether someone came back. They are random values, not derived from your device, and they mean nothing outside Orbit.

    What is never recorded

    • Nothing you write or receive. No birth details, no chart, no reading, no card, no question, no journal entry. An event is a name and a time.
    • No IP address is stored. Orbit limits how often anyone can call its calculation endpoints, and to do that it hashes the network address in memory for a minute at a time. That is abuse protection, it is deliberately kept separate from these counts, and it is never written down.
    • No fingerprinting, no device profiling, no cross-site tracking, and no attempt to identify you when you are signed out.

    Why — to answer four questions while Orbit is in open beta: whether anyone arrives, whether they make an account, whether they use it, and whether they come back. Orbit is being shown to people for the first time, and without these counts there is no way to tell whether it is working for anyone.

    Your choice — if your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, Orbit records none of this. Nothing is counted in a private window either, because the identifiers have nowhere to live. Both are honoured in the code, not just here.

    How long — while you are signed in, these records are linked to your account and are deleted with it. Records from signed-out visits are not linked to any account and stay as counts. Orbit does not yet run an automatic expiry for them, and this page will be updated when it does rather than promising one now.

    Who can see your data

    Only you. Every table holding user data enforces ownership in the database itself, so one account cannot read another's charts, readings, or conversations — that is checked automatically with two separate test accounts, not merely intended.

    How long it is kept

    • Your data is kept while your account exists.
    • Deleting your account removes it permanently. There is no soft delete, no grace period, and no recovery window.
    • Reference data — planetary event tables, tarot card definitions — is not yours and is unaffected.

    Orbit's hosting and database providers may retain their own operational backups for a short period under their standard practice. Orbit does not control those and cannot delete from them on request, so this page does not claim otherwise.

    Tarot reflections are kept only for a signed-in account: the daily card you turn over and any reading you draw are added to your reflections automatically, and today's card is recorded once per day. Signed out, nothing is stored on Orbit's servers — there is no account to keep it for. A saved reflection holds the cards drawn, the question you wrote if you wrote one, and the date — nothing else. Tarot in Orbit Axis is a reflection prompt, not a prediction, and it is not calculated from your chart or from the sky. Saved reflections are included in your data export and are deleted with your account.

    Deleting your account

    In the app: More → Account → Delete account, then type DELETE to confirm. It removes your account, saved charts and birth details, readings and history, Ask Orbit conversations, and preferences, and clears the copy your browser had cached. See how account deletion works.

    Your controls

    • See and edit your saved charts at any time
    • Delete individual charts
    • Change your detail level and preferences
    • Reset your password from the sign-in screen
    • Sign out on any device
    • Delete your entire account permanently
    • Ask a question, or request a copy of your data, at not yet published

    Depending on where you live, you may have further rights under local data protection law. Contact support and Orbit will honour them.

    Age

    Orbit Axis is intended for people aged not yet published and over. It is not directed at children, and accounts are not knowingly created for them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact support and it will be removed.

    Changes to this policy

    If what Orbit collects or how it is used changes, this page is updated and the "last updated" date above changes with it. Material changes will be announced in the app before they take effect.
